# Research

Curated, published research behind archy's design decisions: the literature survey and the empirical studies that gate which metrics ship, get deferred, or get rejected. These are the version-controlled, citable counterparts to raw working notes; they are referenced from the [README](../../README.md), [`ROADMAP.md`](../ROADMAP.md), and [`FUTURE.md`](../FUTURE.md).

| [`AGENT_CAUSAL_REASONING_SYNTHESIS.md`](AGENT_CAUSAL_REASONING_SYNTHESIS.md) | Synthesis of Phroneses' "Agents Cannot Maintain Systems" (additive vs transformative gap), tested against the 2025-2026 causal-reasoning, fitness-function, and AI-PR-review literature. Frames archy as an externalized causal model; maps the article's five causal dimensions to archy's surface (gap analysis); proposes `archy_simulate` (counterfactual pre-edit check) for agent causal reasoning and a per-PR structural review brief for human judgment. Positioning and design input, not new metrics. |
| [`PREWALK_READ_REDUCTION_SYNTHESIS.md`](PREWALK_READ_REDUCTION_SYNTHESIS.md) | Synthesis of Stencil's "Prewalk" (the agent bill is `O(reads)`, not `O(edits)`) against archy's big question (#289): can an archy structural pre-edit brief reduce the exploratory reads an agent does before editing? Concludes prewalk's warm-trajectory handoff is not something a tool-mediated artifact can replicate (archy is weak for the handoff analogue), leaving only the substitution claim, which archy already half-ships (`invariant_brief`, `impact`, `graph(focus)`) but had never been measured. **Outcome: a documented non-result.** The #259 harness's arm-C extension ran to N=22 (`claude-sonnet-5`, flask); the brief did not reliably reduce reads-before-first-edit (pre_edit_reads median Δ −2.0, 14/8 pairs, sign p=0.286, after an N=10 hint at p=0.109 that did not survive more data). **NO-GO on any `archy brief` surface, now on evidence**, not an unproven prior (see `bench/agent_footprint_results.md`). |
| [`CODEGRAPH_COMPETITIVE_ANALYSIS.md`](CODEGRAPH_COMPETITIVE_ANALYSIS.md) | The #316 decision: is archy's Python-only, module-level scope defensible against [codegraph](https://github.com/colbymchenry/codegraph) (symbol-level, 20+ languages, Rust kernel, 62k stars), or is it a gap? **Defensible, and the differentiation is cleaner than expected: the two do different jobs.** codegraph is descriptive (what is here, what calls what) and ships no score, no architecture rules, no cycle analysis, no history, and no counterfactual check; archy is normative (what did you declare, and is it drifting), which needs user-supplied intent rather than better parsing. The overlap is 2 of archy's 11 MCP tools (`archy_impact`, both `blast` and `affected` modes, plus `archy_graph(focus=)`; 3 commands at CLI granularity) and archy loses every one on the merits, which downgrades #270 (typed connectors is symbol-level edge typing in the job archy does not win). Multi-language stays WONTFIX but the reasoning is corrected: archy's metrics are **not** Python-specific, only its parser is, so this is a resourcing call, not a first-principles boundary. Module granularity is a real defense: 4 of the 5 axes degrade or become undefined at symbol level (function-level cycles are just recursion; layers are a package concept). **The uncomfortable finding is that scope was never the problem:** 5 stars / 0 outside issues / empty ADOPTERS after 2.5 months versus 62,279 stars / 385k monthly downloads after 6 is a distribution gap, not a differentiation gap. No new feature tickets, the fourth consecutive research non-result. |
| [`TOKEN_REDUCTION_CLAIMS.md`](TOKEN_REDUCTION_CLAIMS.md) | Survey of the token-reduction claims made by coding-agent tools, harnesses, and skills, and the call on whether archy should build one. **NO-GO.** Sorting the category by *mechanism* splits it in two: **prefix bloat** (tool definitions) is real and independently measured (67,300 tokens across seven MCP servers, 33.7% of a 200k window) and **archy already solved its share** in #227/#265, measuring **3,918 model-visible tokens today, 1.96% of a 200k window**; **comprehension context** (repo maps, briefs, symbol retrieval) is **unmeasured by its own vendors** (aider publishes no ablation of its repo map across any of its four benchmarks; serena asserts efficiency in prose), and the one independent replication that did measure compression reports **42% fewer input tokens for a 12-point drop in resolution rate** at maximum compression (its gentler ablations cost 3 points for 22%). The famous percentages (99.9%, 98.7%, 95%) are computed against baselines nobody runs, and prompt caching (cache hits at 0.1x) already took most of the cost win, overstating real savings by roughly 3-6x over a realistic session for cached prefixes. Also resolves why archy's #282/#289 nulls do not contradict codegraph's strong benchmark: **task class** (their graph answers architecture questions, archy's was a hint for editing tasks). Records a structural-Q&A bench as the reopen path, gated on a usage signal. |
| [`PATTERN_DETECTION_EMPIRICS.md`](PATTERN_DETECTION_EMPIRICS.md) | The #288 decision: can archy *infer* Cosmic Python's architectural patterns (Hexagonal, Repository, Service Layer, Message Bus, UoW/Aggregate/CQRS/DI) from the dependency graph? **NO-GO on evidence.** Four inferred detectors over three corpora (cosmicpython branches / framework-coupled controls / 29 general projects): purity-based is tautological (0 flags across 40 repos), the two position-based ones separate the corpora perfectly at repo level while identifying **zero true domain modules on 2 of the 3 controls** (they flag CLI entrypoints, translation adapters, exception modules) and cannot tell an inapplicable project from a violating one (they report hexagonal violations in django and flask themselves), and naming-based has the best domain precision but is a convention check, 52% vacuous, and blind to transitive coupling. A 14-line `.importlinter` contract with `include_external_packages = True` catches the case they all miss, with chains and line numbers, though on one repo and with the domain names handed to it. **Contracts win on transitivity**; the general reason inference fails is that "domain" is a statement of intent and the import graph does not carry intent. Harness `bench/pattern_detection.py`; the results file logs six corrections made after adversarial review overturned three first-run claims. |
| [`CONSTRAINT_AWARE_ORCHESTRATION_SYNTHESIS.md`](CONSTRAINT_AWARE_ORCHESTRATION_SYNTHESIS.md) | Answer to #298: should archy ship agent-framework integrations (LangGraph, Pydantic AI, CrewAI, OpenAI Agents SDK) to serve the DevDigest/Constraint-Decay call for "constraint-aware orchestration"? Finding: the gap the article names is **archy-side, not integration-side.** All four frameworks are already MCP clients and the remaining guard glue is 10-30 lines each, while the advisory-vs-hard-failure dial the article asks for is only half-built (`sdp.mode: warn\|error` proves the mechanism but is scoped to one built-in heuristic that is off by default; project-authored `forbid` rules have no severity at all). **Native adapters wontfix; docs recipe deferred behind a usage signal (zero inbound demand today); the gate bench not scheduled on cost** (its obvious endpoint is tautological, but three bounded-retry endpoints and a held-out-constraint design are not, all recorded). Two independently-justified archy-side items fall out: per-rule severity (#309) and a canned remediation string (#310). |
| [`INLOOP_PREVALENCE_EMPIRICS.md`](INLOOP_PREVALENCE_EMPIRICS.md) | Empirical answer to the synthesis's deepest open question. Replays 1,072 human-authored commits across 11 mature repos to measure how often a real change introduces a structural regression archy catches. Finding: new import cycles are rare per commit (0.5%) but concentrate in large changes (median 7x the files) and are severe when they fire; the composite score drops on 29% of commits but 98% of drops are sub-0.005 noise (validating the advisory-vs-blocking split). Reframes archy as a rare-firing, low-FP gate; specifies the agent A/B protocol for the remaining causal question. Harness in `bench/inloop_prevalence.py`. |
