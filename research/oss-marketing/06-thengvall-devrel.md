# Mary Thengvall: The Business Value of Developer Relations (2018)

**Source:** Book; companion pieces at https://www.marythengvall.com/blog/2019/12/14/devrel-qualified-leads-repurposing-a-common-business-metrics-to-prove-value and https://thenewstack.io/mary-thengvall-on-measuring-the-value-of-developer-relations/
**Type:** Book (first canonical DevRel text)
**Date accessed:** 2026-05-16

## Key insights

- **"DevRel Qualified Lead" (DQL)** is the load-bearing concept: a lead from the community that gets *routed* to the right team; beta tester → product, great use case → marketing, strong code → recruiting. The DevRel/maintainer's job is matchmaking, not capture.
- DevRel measurement requires **qualitative + quantitative anecdotes** that ladder to company mission. Pure-quantitative ("we did 12 conference talks") doesn't justify budget; pure-qualitative ("people love us") doesn't either.
- Customer feedback routing is core: every community interaction is a chance to surface a real problem to product/support/eng; and those routed signals *are* the DevRel ROI.
- For a one-person OSS project: the "DevRel" function is just the maintainer. The discipline still applies; every issue, comment, DM should be triaged with the DQL lens (does this become a doc, a roadmap item, a testimonial, a partnership?).

## Applicability to archy

- **Adopt a DQL log.** A `research/dql.md` (private or repo) where every external interaction (issue, mention, DM, Slack ping) is logged with: who, what, routed-to-what (README change / roadmap / testimonial / etc.). This is the cheap version of Thengvall's framework for a solo-maintainer project.
- The mental shift: **don't ask "did this conversation get a star"**; ask "did this conversation generate an artifact (doc, fix, roadmap edit, quote)." If yes, the conversation has ROI even at zero stars.
- Specifically for archy: when a Python-architecture conversation happens on Bluesky / r/Python / a Discord, the value isn't "got them to install"; it's whether their question becomes a `docs/FAQ.md` entry, a benchmark case, a roadmap item, or a quotable testimonial.
- **Testimonials are undervalued.** One in-context quote from a recognizable engineer ("archy caught the layer violation our reviewer missed") is worth more than 100 stars. Solicit explicitly when users say something positive; "would you mind if I quoted this in the README?"
